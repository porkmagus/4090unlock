# AD102 cross-SKU policy: consumer vs professional tensor rate

**Question:** does a professional AD102 GPU achieve different effective Tensor
throughput because of hardware fuse configuration, authenticated firmware
initialization, or both?

**Answer in one paragraph.** The professional AD102 does run the FP32-accumulate
tensor paths at **twice the per-SM, per-clock rate** of the RTX 4090, and the
difference is a **configuration state, not extra silicon**: the same die in the
same generation is sold at both rates (L40 vs RTX 6000 Ada, identical SM count
and clock, 2× apart), and the RTX 4090 itself reaches the professional per-clock
rate after two host register writes. Which mechanism *seeds* that state — OTP
fuse words or authenticated firmware — is not yet proven, but every observable
piece of evidence points at fuse-programmed hardware defaults, and none at
firmware.

Machine-readable companion: `research/ad102_sku_matrix.csv`
(generated by `research/make_sku_matrix.py`).

---

## 1. Method (and why raw TFLOPS are useless here)

Comparing advertised TFLOPS cannot distinguish "bigger card" from "different
rate per SM". Every number below is normalised:

```
FLOP per SM per clock = TFLOPS × 1e12 / (SM count × boost clock in MHz × 1e6)
```

Sources are the two NVIDIA architecture whitepapers, which state the
accumulate type explicitly:

* **Consumer:** *NVIDIA Ada GPU Architecture* v2.02, Appendix A, Table 2
  (GeForce RTX 4090 full specifications).
* **Professional:** *NVIDIA Ada Lovelace Professional GPU Architecture* v1.1,
  Appendix A, Table 2 (RTX 6000 Ada Generation full specifications) and
  Appendix C, Table 4 (L40 vs A40).

Both tables are dense (sparsity excluded) and both use the same footnote
convention, so the accumulate-type split is directly comparable.

| Path | 4090 FLOP/SM/clk | RTX 6000 Ada FLOP/SM/clk | Ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| FP8, FP16 acc | 2048 | 2048 | 1.00 |
| FP8, **FP32 acc** | **1024** | **2048** | **2.00** |
| FP16, FP16 acc | 1024 | 1024 | 1.00 |
| FP16, **FP32 acc** | **512** | **1024** | **2.00** |
| BF16, **FP32 acc** | **512** | **1024** | **2.00** |
| TF32 | **256** | **512** | **2.00** |
| INT8 | 2048 | 2048 | 1.00 |
| FP32 vector | 256 | 256 | 1.00 |

Every path that *accumulates in FP32* is halved on the GeForce, and only those
paths. FP16-accumulate FP8/FP16, INT8 and vector FP32 are identical per SM.

That is the same signature as `FEAT_READOUT_1 = 0x00410080` at cold on a 4090:
exactly **three** of nine per-unit fields are non-zero (`FMLA32`, `IMLA2`,
`IMLA4`, each value 2 = reduced), which lines up with the three FP32-accumulate
tensor paths NVIDIA rates at half — FP16/BF16-FP32acc, FP8-FP32acc and TF32.
The field-to-unit mapping is **INFERRED** (fuse order, one field per unit); the
count and the pattern are **VERIFIED** against the published cold capture. See
`docs/07` for how to confirm it on hardware.

### 2.4 The control that kills the "consumer vs pro" story

| Card | Die | SMs | Boost | FP16 dense | FP16 per SM/clk | INT8 dense | INT8 per SM/clk |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| GeForce RTX 4090 | AD102 | 128 | 2520 | 330.3 (FP16 acc) | 1024 | 660.6 | 2048 |
| **NVIDIA L40** | **AD102** | **142** | **2490** | **181.0** | **512** | **362.0** | **1024** |
| **RTX 6000 Ada** | **AD102** | **142** | **2505** | **364.2** | **1024** | **728.5** | **2048** |

L40 and RTX 6000 Ada are the **same die, same SM count, same clock**, and differ
by exactly 2× on every tensor path — including INT8, which has no
accumulate-type split at all. The L40's FP32 rating (90.5 TFLOPS over 18,176
cores) puts its boost at 2.49 GHz, within 0.6 % of the RTX 6000 Ada's 2.505 GHz.

So the half rate is **not** a consumer/professional segmentation and **not** a
silicon bin: NVIDIA sells the same AD102 at two tensor rates inside its own
professional line. *L40 source: consumer whitepaper v2.02, Appendix C, Table 4;
clock derived from FP32 TFLOPS ÷ (CUDA cores × 2).* **VERIFIED (whitepaper) /
INFERRED (derived clock).**

## 3. Independent measurement (not ours)

A third-party measurement published 2026-10-28 (`airealist.ai`, "GeForce vs
Workstation GPUs: NVIDIA's Half Rate, Priced") ran cuBLAS FP16 GEMM with FP16
and FP32 accumulation on rented cards and normalised by the median SM clock:

| Card | FP32-acc ÷ FP16-acc, per clock | Runs |
|---|---:|---|
| GeForce RTX 4090 (AD102) | **0.52 – 0.55** | 2 hosts |
| RTX 6000 Ada (AD102) | **0.88 – 1.04** | 2 hosts |
| RTX A6000 (GA102) | 0.96 – 1.06 | 2 hosts |
| GeForce RTX 3090 (GA102) | 0.51 – 0.54 | 2 hosts |

It also measured delivered throughput at stock power limits: RTX 4090
145.0–163.1 TFLOPS (FP32 accumulate) against RTX 6000 Ada 93.3–127.1 — the
professional card is **slower in practice** because its 300 W limit holds it
near 960–975 MHz, while the 4090 sustains ~2.5 GHz at 450 W.

Two conclusions follow, and they matter for the acceptance design:

1. The spec-sheet 2× is a **per-clock** property. It survives at the same power
   only partially, which is why the unlock's application-level gain is reported
   as 10–34 %, not 2×.
2. The professional card is not a faster product for sustained GEMM. Its
   advantage is the rate setting; its disadvantage is the power cap. An
   unlocked 4090 keeps 450 W **and** the full rate.

**OBSERVED (third-party, with published method and raw results).** Not
independently reproduced here; no GPU was available.

| FWSEC `0x85` SHA-256 (content) | `a9dbe61166d858be…` | not obtained | VERIFIED / UNKNOWN |
| Board power limit | 450 W (480 W max) | 300 W (not raiseable) | VERIFIED / OBSERVED |

The decisive firmware comparison — is the professional card's FWSEC byte-identical
to the consumer card's — **could not be made**: TechPowerUp serves the RTX 6000
Ada VBIOS page but blocks the `.rom` download from this host (HTTP 403 on the
page scrape and on every direct download path tried), and no other trustworthy
mirror was found. It is recorded as the cheapest follow-up in §7.

What can be said without it:

* The ROM side of this card contains **four signed descriptors** covering two
  firmware applications in debug/production pairs (`docs/05` §3). Nothing in
  them is SKU-specific in *structure*; the only SKU identity in the FWSEC DMEM
  is the PCI device word `0x2684` at DMEM offset 8.
* The VBIOS is **not** a rate table. It contains no speed-select write in any
  plaintext region, and the decompressed GOP driver reads the fuse block only
  for memory sizing.
* Therefore, if the professional card's rate comes from its VBIOS at all, it
  would have to come from inside encrypted FWSEC — and the same FWSEC is what
  the 4090 runs. That is possible but has no supporting evidence.

## 6. Answer to the critical question

**"Does a professional AD102 GPU achieve different effective Tensor throughput
because of hardware fuse configuration, authenticated firmware initialization,
or both?"**

What is established:

1. **Yes, different throughput** — 2× per SM per clock on exactly the
   FP32-accumulate paths, and 2× across the board between L40 and RTX 6000 Ada.
2. **It is a state, not silicon** — the 4090 reaches the professional per-clock
   rate after two host register writes (`SS0 ← 0x88888888`, `SS1 ← 0x8`), which
   the same silicon honours at full rate.
3. **It is not consumer-vs-professional** — L40 (professional) runs the consumer
   rate.

What is not established: whether the *default* state is loaded from OTP fuse
words by hardware, or written by authenticated firmware that reads a SKU fuse.

Weight of evidence:

| Observation | Favours |
|---|---|
| Effective readout at cold matches the fuse words field-for-field (3 reduced units ↔ 3 fuses at 1) | fuse-derived hardware default |
| No plaintext firmware (legacy x86, GOP PE, PRE_OS, LS_UDE, app 0x07) writes or reads `0x8238xx` | fuse-derived |
| Falcon firmware *does* read `0x0082xxxx` words (`0x821304`, `0x8215AC`) — the aperture is live at boot | neutral: proves firmware consumes fuse data, not that it sets the rate |
| Two SKUs of the same die and clock differ 2× in a shipped product | a per-SKU configuration input exists; fuses are the conventional one |
| FWSEC is encrypted; its side effects are unobservable | firmware-mediated seeding cannot be excluded |

**Best-supported conclusion:** the rate is a **hardware-configured default**
(fuse/strap-programmed speed-select state), and the professional SKUs differ
because their fuses are programmed differently. Firmware-mediated seeding
remains **possible but unsupported**: it requires the same encrypted FWSEC to
behave differently per SKU, and the only SKU identifier visible in that
firmware's plaintext DMEM is the PCI device id.

Confidence: **INFERRED, high** for "configuration state rather than silicon";
**INFERRED, medium** for "fuse rather than firmware"; **UNKNOWN** until a
professional card is read.

## 7. The one experiment that settles it

Read `FUSE_SS_FMLA32` (`0x008207D8`) and `FEAT_READOUT_1` (`0x00823818`) on an
**RTX 6000 Ada or L40**, cold, with no driver bound — using the read-only kit in
`docs/07` (UEFI path or Linux live USB). Nothing is written.

| Observation | Conclusion |
|---|---|
| `FUSE_SS_FMLA32 = 0` and readout fields all 0 | **Fuse origin confirmed.** The professional card's full rate is a fuse-programmed default; no firmware patch is needed to explain it, and the 4090's cap is OTP. |
| `FUSE_SS_FMLA32 = 1` but readout is 0 and the card benchmarks at ~1024 FLOP/SM/clk | **Fuse origin refuted.** The rate is seeded by something other than that fuse word — most likely authenticated firmware or a strap — and the VBIOS/FWSEC path becomes the primary suspect. |
| Fuses identical to the 4090 *and* readout identical (`0x00410080`) | The readout is not the effective rate; the whole decode in this document must be revised before any persistence plan proceeds. |

The same capture also answers whether the professional card's `SS0`/`SS1`
differ from the consumer's, which bounds any "write the pro values" idea.
